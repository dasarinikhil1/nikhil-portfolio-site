Summer 2025, Dallas

Technology Consulting Intern, EY

Supported an ECC to S/4HANA transformation: analyzed access controls, mitigated segregation-of-duties risk, and translated stakeholder needs into 10+ clear business requirements that guided leadership decisions.

Returning full time as a Technology Consultant

The work

This summer, I had the opportunity to intern with EY as a Technology Consulting Intern in Dallas. Going into the internship, I knew I wanted to better understand what technology consulting actually looked like beyond the surface level, and the experience gave me a much clearer picture of how large-scale technology decisions affect an entire organization.

I worked on an SAP transformation engagement for NVIDIA, supporting its transition from SAP ECC to S/4HANA. A lot of my work focused on SAP GRC, including areas such as access controls, segregation-of-duties risks, business-process requirements, and governance. I also spent time understanding the client's existing ECC environment and helping document how different processes and requirements would translate into the future S/4HANA system.

One of the biggest things I took away from the internship was realizing how much goes into a technology transformation beyond simply implementing a new system. Every technical change can affect the people using it, the company's internal controls, existing business processes, and the risks the organization needs to manage. I enjoyed being able to look at those problems from both a technical and business perspective.

I also had the chance to work closely with senior consultants and contribute to client-facing documentation and deliverables. That experience pushed me to think more carefully about how I communicate technical information. It wasn't enough to understand the problem myself; I had to be able to explain it clearly and organize the information in a way that was useful to other people.

Overall, my time at EY gave me a much stronger understanding of enterprise technology, consulting, and technology risk. More importantly, it helped me become more comfortable working through ambiguous problems, asking better questions, and understanding how technology fits into the bigger picture of a business.

2025 to 2026, Atlanta

Product Development Co-op, Delta Air Lines

Built allotment automation for DeliverDirect in Python and VBA, scaling operations across 500+ transactions and multiple teams. Root-cause analysis in Tableau improved SLA compliance by 10% across 20+ last-mile stations.

The work

During Spring 2025, I had the opportunity to work as a Product Development Co-op with Delta Air Lines in Atlanta. The experience gave me a much better understanding of how technology, data, and product decisions come together in a large operational environment.

A major part of my work focused on Delta Cargo and DeliverDirect, where I helped investigate issues across last-mile delivery operations. I spent time auditing Air Waybill data, identifying recurring process failures, and understanding where manual work was creating bottlenecks. After learning the process in detail, I worked on automating parts of the workflow using Python and VBA, including tools that helped make shipment auditing and reconciliation more efficient.

I also worked on analyzing delivery failures using Tableau and PULSE data across multiple last-mile stations. One of the most interesting parts of this work was going beyond the numbers and trying to understand why certain failures were happening. My analysis helped surface recurring patterns and supported decisions around product improvements and backlog prioritization.

Another important project involved a revenue reconciliation issue tied to DeliverDirect. I worked with shipment and invoicing data to identify discrepancies after a process failure resulted in missing revenue true-ups. This gave me exposure to how operational and technical issues can quickly become financial problems, and it reinforced the importance of having reliable systems and clear processes in place.

What I enjoyed most about the internship was the variety of problems I was able to work on. Some days involved digging through operational data, while others involved building automation, collaborating with different teams, or preparing findings for stakeholders. I had the opportunity to work across product, operations, finance, and technology, which helped me understand how interconnected each part of the business really is.

Overall, my time at Delta taught me how to approach ambiguous problems, look for opportunities to automate repetitive work, and use data to make better product decisions. It also showed me that meaningful technical work is not always about building a completely new system; sometimes the biggest impact comes from understanding an existing process deeply and finding a better way to make it work.

Summer 2024

Generative AI Extern, Cognizant

Designed NLP and CNN models in PyTorch, improving accuracy through transfer learning and hyperparameter tuning.

The work

I had the opportunity to participate in Cognizant's Generative AI Externship, a virtual program focused on building practical skills in Python, artificial intelligence, and generative AI. The program combined self-paced technical training with hands-on projects, giving me a chance to go beyond learning AI concepts in the classroom and actually see how they could be applied.

Throughout the externship, I was able to strengthen my understanding of machine learning and deep learning while getting more exposure to areas such as neural networks, pretrained models, and model fine-tuning. Similar projects within the program involved working with image-classification models and adapting pretrained language models for new use cases, which helped connect the theory behind AI with the process of actually building and improving models.

What I enjoyed most about the experience was being able to explore a field that was changing so quickly. Generative AI was becoming increasingly important across the technology industry, and the externship gave me an opportunity to understand more of what was happening behind the applications I was seeing every day rather than only interacting with AI from a user's perspective.

The program also pushed me to become more comfortable learning unfamiliar technologies independently. Working through technical material, debugging problems, and experimenting with different approaches reinforced how important curiosity and continuous learning are when working in AI.

Overall, my experience with Cognizant helped strengthen my interest in artificial intelligence and gave me a stronger technical foundation that I could carry into future software engineering and AI projects. More than anything, it encouraged me to keep exploring how AI systems can be built and used to solve real-world problems.
